# English Style Patterns: Clarity, Purpose, and Voice

Use these prompts to explain reading problems, not to classify human/AI authorship or predict moderation. Preserve claims, evidence, quotations, citations, links, and necessary authorship or AI-use disclosures. Correct grammar and spelling are welcome in every genre.

## Structure

Keep headings, lists, numbered steps, and quote-then-respond formats when they help readers. Merge repetitive points or move the answer earlier when useful. A comment may be brief; a technical explanation may need several paragraphs. Articles can have introductions and conclusions. There is no universal paragraph limit.

## Openings and identity

“This resonates” or “As someone who…” may be relevant or expendable. Ask whether the opening adds information. Remove irrelevant setup without inventing credentials, a team, or a personal story. Do not convert product facts into a fabricated user testimonial.

## Body and evidence

- Make unclear referents and relationships explicit.
- Keep balanced arguments when the evidence warrants them. Do not force a side or weaken a factual statement to simulate uncertainty.
- Retain examples with distinct evidentiary value; trim repetition rather than enforcing an example count.
- Use “I” or “we” according to the actual speaker, not as a style trick.
- Keep technical terms and bilingual passages when appropriate for the audience.
- Use formal connectors when they express a useful relationship. Cut empty transitions only when the logic remains clear.

Fictional editing example (no personal experience implied):

> Before: In order to make it possible to restore the files, it is necessary to create a backup first.
>
> After: Back up the files so they can be restored.

## Endings

A conclusion, a specific question, or encouragement can fit the purpose. Remove unsupported uplift or repetition when it distracts. Do not force a trailing fragment, fake hesitation, or an unfinished thought.

## Surface and rhythm

Correct spelling, apostrophes, em dashes, semicolons, colons, and complete sentences are all permitted. Never insert typos, filler quotas, “human fingerprints,” or deliberate grammatical errors. Break difficult sentences or combine choppy ones according to meaning, not a target length distribution. Repetition may be a deliberate rhetorical choice.

## Genre and publication context

| Genre | Editing focus |
|---|---|
| Comments and short posts | Answer the point clearly; keep useful supporting detail |
| Articles and documentation | Preserve hierarchy, evidence, procedures, and qualifications |
| Literary prose and poetry | Discuss imagery, narrative, and voice; preserve intentional line breaks |

Platform names do not prescribe a fixed tone or an assumed detection regime. Verify current platform requirements separately when relevant. Ordinary editing of an AI draft is allowed; retain its disclosure. For requests to evade detection or conceal necessary disclosure, state the boundary briefly and offer normal editing help.
